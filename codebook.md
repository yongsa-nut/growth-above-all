# Codebook

**Unit.** One list = one situation and the six items a model wrote for it. Code each item from its **header, or its first sentence if there is no header**; the rest of the item only settles what the header leaves open.

**What gets coded.** Every item gets an item type (Q1). A consolation also gets a consolation type (Q1b). A lining also gets a goal (Q3) and a beneficiary (Q4). Actions and items that are not linings stop at Q1.

---

## Q1 · Item type: what does this item give the asker?

Three of the four answers are ways of coping. A **lining** and a **consolation** both look at the event differently: a lining finds a gain in it that serves a goal, a consolation eases the sting without naming a gain. An **action** tells the asker what to do rather than how to see the event.

Ask in order; the first yes decides.

| Order | Question | Code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Is it off-task: a story angle or writing prompt (how to tell the event, not what it gives), refusal or meta ("I'm sorry you're going through this"), off-topic, or a general life tip with no link to the event? | **not a lining** |
| 2 | Does it **tell the asker to take an outward step**: an instruction or a recommendation, with the asker as the one who acts (ask the coach, try again, join a club, you should talk to them, you could suggest another day; ลอง… ควร…)? An instruction to see or feel differently is not an action. | **action** |
| 3 | Does it find a gain **in the event itself**: something the asker, or people around them, got, kept, avoided, learned, or can do because of what happened, a good result it produced, or what the event itself offers while it happens? | **lining** |
| 4 | Does it only change how the asker should see or feel about the event: not your fault, not about you, only one test, could have been worse, there is still time, this will pass, your worth is unchanged, meant to be? | **consolation** |
| 5 | Otherwise: it retells the event with nothing good named, or names a feature of it with nothing the feature did for anyone. | **not a lining** |

Rules:

- **Told to do vs a chance opened.** An action tells or recommends the asker a step: "ask the coach for feedback", "try again with better timing", "you could suggest another day". A chance the event opens is a lining, even when the asker would have to act on it: "a chance to widen your circle", "it may open room to meet new friends", "a chance to talk frankly with your friends", "a chance to do other things instead".
- **Carrying on is not an action.** Doing what the asker was going to do anyway is part of the event: "if you go to the film on your own, you can pick your seat" → lining.
- **A possibility the item only notes is not an action**: "your old books can go to your younger sibling" → lining.
- **An action is an outward step.** An instruction to see, accept or feel differently is not an action. When it names a gain, code the gain ("be proud of reaching the semifinal" → lining, achievement; "be proud of how much you improved" → lining, growth); when it names none, it is a consolation ("accept it and let it go", "don't blame yourself"). Seeing the event differently is not an action either ("a chance to practice not taking it personally" → lining).
- **Gain vs reframe.** When the lead clause names something the asker now has, it is a lining even if the rest reassures ("It reveals useful information, not your worth" → lining). When it only reframes, it is a consolation ("It's not about you").
- **Feature vs gain.** The convenience of the tool or medium the event used (an app, a chat, a payment system) is a feature, not a gain: "using iMessage was convenient, so you didn't need to meet in person" and "you didn't have to go to an ATM, you paid from bed" → not a lining. A gain has to come from the event itself, and one that does still counts when the item mentions the medium ("the money is settled, so everyone sleeps easy" → lining).
- **Gains for people around the asker count** ("everyone had a chance to share ideas", "gear day made everyone feel like an official team") → lining. The beneficiary question records whose gain it is.
- **An insight is a lining.** "You got to see that winning and losing come and go, nothing is permanent" names something gained → lining (transcendent). "This will pass" names nothing gained → consolation (impermanence).
- **"At least" is a consolation.** An item that opens with a comparison to something worse ("at least…", "better than…", "it could have been worse") → consolation (could be worse), even if it names something gained, unless it says what the asker can now do with it ("at least you found out now, so you get to make your own plan" → lining). This rule comes before row 3. A comparison that follows a gain does not change the code.
- **A good result is a gain, even when it is the outcome.** An item that names a good result of the event ("you finished in the top ten", "you made the varsity squad", "your band reached the regional round") → lining; Q3 gives its goal (a result that tested ability or effort is achievement). An item that opens with "at least" follows the rule above. Only a retelling with nothing good named is not a lining ("the game ended 54 to 60", "you went to the tryout").
- **"Still time" is a consolation.** An item that says the chance is not lost ("there is still time", "there is still a chance to make up for it", "there will be other tryouts") → consolation (impermanence), even when it names what the asker could do then. Time or a chance named as something the asker now has ("you have time to build your skills before the next opportunity", "you might get to invite others next time") → lining.
- **A vague hint is not a gain.** "Everything happens to teach you something" → consolation.

## Q1b · Consolation type  *(consolation only)*

- **acceptance**: it is what it is; letting go; ทำใจ; ไม่เป็นไร
- **impermanence**: this will pass; there is still time; next time
- **could be worse**: at least…; better than…
- **reassurance**: not your fault; not about you; your worth is unchanged; you'll be fine
- **fate / meaning**: meant to be; everything happens for a reason; karma
- **other**

## Q3 · Goal: what kind of gain is it?  *(lining only)*

The goal is the kind of gain, for whoever gains it: "your teacher doesn't have to chase you" is wellbeing, and the beneficiary question records that the gain is the teacher's. One exception: what family or friends feel because of the asker (proud, happy, relieved) is relational. Code the gain the lead clause names. If it names two, take the first that applies in this order.

| Order | Code | The gain | Coarse level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **transcendent** | meaning beyond the event: fate, karma, merit, "do good and good comes back", "meant to be", a Buddhist truth seen (nothing is permanent) | beyond |
| 2 | **community** | service to, pride for, or representing a collective beyond the asker's own relationships: the school, the province, younger students | social |
| 3 | **relational** | a connection between people: closer, trust, belonging, friendship, fairness or harmony within the group, not burdening friends, family or the group, gratitude repaid (repaying your parents), face for the family, family or friends proud, happy or relieved because of the asker, knowing who your real friends are | social |
| 4 | **growth** | the student got better or did something new: a skill, a lesson, strength, resilience, a first, knowledge, proof to themselves of what they can do, or effort that paid off with no outcome named | self |
| 5 | **achievement** | a positive outcome or recognition the item names: a win, a place, a grade, being selected, a spot earned, praise or recognition by others | self |
| 6 | **wellbeing** | a feeling or a lighter mind: fun, excitement, rest, relaxing, relief, less stress, less hassle, one less thing to think about, closure, a memory of a good time | self |
| 7 | **resources** *(name open)* | the student's situation is better arranged: time, money, things in order, a record kept, a step done, having what you need, an opportunity in hand | self |
| – | **unspecified** | no gain can be read | – |

Rules:

- **Growth vs achievement.** Growth is a change in the student: they got better, learned, or did something new. Achievement is a good result the event produced. A quality the event showed ("you showed commitment", "you showed real discipline") is growth: proof to the student of what they can do. Achievement needs the outcome or the recognition named in the item ("you won", "you placed", "you earned your spot", "the coach recognized your work"); effort that "paid off", "counted" or "was worth it", with no outcome named, is growth. When the item names the outcome, it is achievement even if the lead clause is the effort ("your hard work paid off: you got an A" → achievement).
- **Knowledge goes to growth**, except knowledge about a relationship, which is relational ("you see who your real friends are", "you learned how much this group matters to you").
- **Wellbeing vs resources.** A feeling, or a mind spared worry, stress or hassle, is wellbeing, for whoever is spared it ("your teacher doesn't have to chase you" → wellbeing, beneficiary other). Time, money, a trip or step saved, a record and things in order, with nothing said about the mind, are resources. "A free Friday night" → resources. "Time to go home and relax" → wellbeing. A task done goes by what the item says it spares: "one less thing to think about" → wellbeing; "an important step done for the season" → resources.
- **Achievement vs resources.** A result that tested ability or effort is achievement. A step done with no test in it (paperwork in, gear picked up) is resources, or wellbeing when the item names the relief.
- **Memories** take the code of what they are a memory of: of a win → achievement, with friends → relational, of a good day → wellbeing.
- **Family and friends' feelings are relational.** Parents or friends who are proud, happy or relieved because of the asker: relational, beneficiary other ("your parents are thrilled", "your parents can stop worrying"). The same feeling in someone outside those relationships goes by its kind ("your teacher doesn't have to chase you" → wellbeing).
- **Not imposing on people is relational.** Not having to burden, chase or tiptoe around friends, family or the group (เกรงใจ) is relational, even when worded as a feeling ("nobody has to feel awkward chasing anyone for money" → relational). The same relief for someone outside those relationships goes by its kind ("your teacher doesn't have to chase you" → wellbeing).
- **Relational vs community.** A connection, a debt of gratitude, or honour between the asker and particular people (friends, parents, the family) is relational. Service to, pride for or representing a collective beyond those relationships (the school, the province, younger students) is community.

## Q4 · Beneficiary: whose gain is it?  *(lining only)*

- **self**: the asker, including gains they get through a relationship ("you'll feel closer to them")
- **other**: a specific other person's state ("your parents will be proud", "your friend gets a turn")
- **group**: the team, the friend group, the family, the school as a unit
- **transcendent order**: karma, fate, the universe, a deity
- **unspecified**: no beneficiary can be read

Rules: the beneficiary is who gains, not who is mentioned. A **shared good** → group: a friendship or relationship strengthened, the team closer or working better, a shared memory, fairness or trust within the group, something the group handled together, pride brought to the school, representing the school. A good that lands on the asker alone → self, even when others are involved: feeling closer to them, more confident, respected, included, welcomed, rested. "Your teammates gained confidence" → group; "your teammates respect you more" → self; "you'll feel closer to them" → self; "your friendships get stronger" → group. Two beneficiaries → the main clause. Thai เรา is I or we by context, not a group cue on its own.

## Coarse goal level (computed, not coded)

self = growth, achievement, wellbeing, resources · social = relational, community · beyond = transcendent.
